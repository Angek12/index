Good produce and empty shelves rarely meet.

Shop owners guess what to reorder, so popular items run out while others go unsold. Farmers have a harvest but no clear view of who needs it. Index puts both sides in one place, with clear numbers.

Stockouts

Shops lose sales when a popular crop runs out before the next delivery.

Unsold produce

Stock that nobody asked for spoils, and the money behind it is lost.

No view for farmers

Farmers cannot see what shops need, in what amount, or by when.

How Index works.

Four steps take an order from a shelf gap to a recorded delivery. Everything happens in the official portal, on a phone or a computer.

Open the portal

  1. Shops ask

    A shop records its sales and requests the crop, quantity and date it needs.

  2. Farmers offer

    A farmer lists what is ready, how much, at what price and until when.

  3. A coordinator matches

    The coordinator sees the best available farmers for each request and creates the order.

  4. Everyone tracks

    The farmer confirms and delivers, the shop confirms receipt, and every step is saved.

The life of an order
StatusWho actsWhat happens

Questions and answers.

How do I join?

Open the official portal, choose Create an account, and pick whether you are a shop or a farmer. It takes a few minutes.

Does Index handle payments?

No. Index records the agreed price, quantity and delivery date. Payment is arranged directly between the shop and the farmer.

What happens if an order fails?

The person who cancels or marks it failed must write a reason. The reserved quantity goes back to the farmer and the shop's request opens again.

How does the forecast work?

It averages recent weeks of recorded sales to estimate next week, then compares that estimate with simply repeating last week, and shows which one is more accurate. It needs a few weeks of sales to start.

Can I use Index on my phone?

Yes. The portal opens in any phone browser, and an Android app is available for pilot users.

Is my information protected?

Passwords are stored scrambled, each person sees only their own records, and farmers never see shop names in the market demand view.

Which crops are included?

The starter list has tomatoes, onions, Irish potatoes, cabbage, carrots, green peppers and bananas. Coordinators can add more.

Where Index is today.

Index is in its pilot stage, starting with shops in Kigali and farmers who supply them. These improvements are planned next.
